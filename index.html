Item 01 · Agenda

Built from the paper trail of a working day.

The attested copy. The stamp that makes it official. The memo nobody reads twice. The spreadsheet that runs the quarter. The file that moves from desk to desk.

This is the material of Indian working life: unglamorous, precise, everywhere. Good Indian borrows its look, its rhythm and its honesty. Never its bureaucracy.

The result doesn’t dress up as an office. It just feels like it’s been somewhere.

Item 02 · For your approval

Filled in, one character per box.

Every Indian professional has filled in a form that says: write in BLOCK LETTERS, one character per box.

The Good Indian wordmark is set the same way: capitals on an equal grid, each letter given its own box of space.

Calm, official, filled in carefully.

Primary wordmark logo

Two holes frame it, punched in the margin exactly where a file punch lands, one over the first letter of INDIAN, one over the last. They mark the full width of the name, the way punch holes mark the width of a file.

Filled in block letters, one character per box. Punched, and ready to file.

Item 03 · Attested

The signature of the brand is a stamp.

In India, you vouch for a document by signing across it and stamping it. So we do the same for every pair: specs on the flap, a real signature, a real stamp, pressed by hand.

The only mark allowed to be imperfect, because it’s the only one made by hand, on every box.

Inspected. Signed. Stamped. A pair isn’t ready until it’s attested.

Item 06 · PFA

The box opens like a document should: facts first.

Item 07 · Annexures A–E

Everything he needs. Nothing he’ll skim.

Care, fit, the guarantee, returns, and his place in the first run, bound like a file, tabbed like a binder, separated by tracing-paper drawings of the last.

The returns slip comes in triplicate. Because some habits are worth keeping.

Item 08 · Circulated

Seven posters.
Seven pieces of the working day.

Each poster borrows one document from the paper trail he already lives in: the spreadsheet, the timesheet, the edit, the attestation, the seating plan, the register, the draft. Then it says one true thing about the shoe in that document’s language.

No faces to aspire to. No slogans to live up to.
Just the working day, and a shoe that keeps up with it.
